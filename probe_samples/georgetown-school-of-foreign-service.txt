Climate Change and Rural Livelihoods in Naryn, Kyrgyzstan

Dr. Azamat Azarov (University of Central Asia) explores how climate change is reshaping natural resources and agrarian life in Kyrgyzstan’s Naryn region, combining environmental analysis with gender-sensitive research. Indranil Chakravarty, “The Power of Intercultural Dialogue: Octavio Paz between Mexico and India”

Based on his recent book, The Tree Within: Octavio Paz’s Years in India (Penguin, 2025), Indranil Chakravarty discusses how the Mexican Nobel Laureate poet and diplomat, Octavio Paz, became a cultural bridge between two parts of the Global South that are separated by geography, language, culture and history.
As Mexico’s ambassador to India in the 1960s, Paz immersed himself in the country’s contemplative traditions, travelled widely, forged deep friendships with some of India’s finest minds, and produced several of his most inspired poetry and essays. By his own admission, he experienced a ‘second birth’.
Exploring the poet’s inner journey by delving into multilingual archives, declassified diplomatic files, personal letters and intimate interviews, Chakravarty explains how Paz prepared, emotionally and intellectually, for a deep engagement with India, allowing it to reshape himself. While he gave poetic form to several Indian philosophical concepts and became a mentor to an entire generation of avant garde painters, he opened pathways through which Latin American writers and artists could draw inspiration from Indian culture.

Dr. Chakrabarty will be in conversation with Professors Nicolas Campisi (Dept of Spanish and Portuguese) and Supriya Goswami (SFS Program in Culture and Politics).

Indranil Chakravarty is a writer, filmmaker and academic based in India. His main research interest is in tracing cultural interactions between India and Latin America. For more than two decades, he has been a professor of film history at India’s premiere film institutes and has made several documentaries. He studied at Presidency College (Calcutta), Escuela Internacional de Cine y TV (Havana) and received PhD from Victoria University of Wellington. His publications include The New Latin American Cinema, India’s Audiovisual Market, and one in Spanish titled Redescubriendo a Tagore, about the discourse around the Indian poet in Hispanic literature. His next book, Hometowns (2027), is the biography of a 19th-century Indian villager who led Brazilian battalions in two major wars.

A reception will follow.

The event is co-sponsored by the Georgetown Humanities Initiative, the SFS Program in Culture and Politics, the Center for Latin-American Studies, the Department of Spanish and Portuguese, and the Department of History.

After the Holocaust: New Histories of Soviet Jews, 1945–1953

In 1945, Soviet Jews emerged from the Holocaust into a country that had defeated Nazism but would soon turn against them. In this conversation, historians Diana Dumitru (Georgetown University) and Anna Shternshis (University of Toronto) discuss their recent books on the years between victory and Stalin’s death: how Jews returned to devastated hometowns and rebuilt families, communities, and careers, and how they navigated a state that both rewarded their achievements and suppressed their culture and identity. Moderated by Charles King (Georgetown University).

Books:

Diana Dumitru, Soviet Jews under Late Stalinism: A Story from the Western Borderlands (Cambridge University Press, 2026)

Anna Shternshis, Jews in the Soviet Union: A History, Volume 4: Postwar Life, Hopes, and Fears, 1945–1953 (NYU Press, 2026)

Climate, Development, and Generational Change in Gilgit-Baltistan and Chitral

Qudsia Shah (The Institute of Ismaili Studies) explores how communities in Gilgit-Baltistan and Chitral understand nature through folklore and local narratives, tracing how these relationships have evolved alongside the region’s developmental transformations. It then turns to Gen Z voices, examining how young people navigate questions of climate change and migration, revealing development as an ongoing, contested process shaped by emerging challenges and aspirations.
