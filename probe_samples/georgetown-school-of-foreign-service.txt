Far-Right France: How the Rise of Marine Le Pen and Jordan Bardella Will Transform Europe and NATO

Right-wing populists and nationalists have been sweeping into power across the world in recent years, from Brexit Britain to the US under Donald Trump, from Hungary with Viktor Orbán to Argentina with Javier Milei.

France is likely to be one of the next countries to fall under the sway of the far-right, which will have incalculable consequences for its European neighbours and for the world. Since the 1980s, author and journalist Victor Mallet has observed the inexorable rise of the anti-immigration Front National – now the Rassemblement National – first under the late Jean-Marie Le Pen and now under his daughter Marine Le Pen, who recently anointed her young and charismatic protégé Jordan Bardella as successor.

Far-Right France: Le Pen, Bardella and the Future of Europe explains how they began to dominate French politics and asks what happens next in a liberal democracy that was one of the founders of the European Union and is a crucial member of the Nato alliance confronting Russia.

International Student Listening Session

International students are central to the intellectual life of SFS. Their perspectives strengthen our classrooms, our research, and our community, reflected in maps pinned with the homes of students spread across 116 countries on six continents.

The SFS Office of Diversity, Equity and Inclusion invites the SFS international community to a listening session on Wednesday, October 21, from 4:00 to 5:00 p.m. in the ICC 700 Conference Room. Dean Joel Hellman and Vice Dean Carla Koppell will host the session to hear students’ questions, concerns, and experiences. The goal is to offer a space to receive information, ask questions, and share how recent policy developments are affecting the community, so that SFS can continue to strengthen its support. Attendees are encouraged to RSVP and to submit any questions they would like addressed using the linked form.

Climate Change and Rural Livelihoods in Naryn, Kyrgyzstan

Dr. Azamat Azarov (University of Central Asia) explores how climate change is reshaping natural resources and agrarian life in Kyrgyzstan’s Naryn region, combining environmental analysis with gender-sensitive research. Highlighting differentiated impacts on women and men, the study reveals climate change as both an ecological and social process that reconfigures vulnerability and adaptation in rural communities.
