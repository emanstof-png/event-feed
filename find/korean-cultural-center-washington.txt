Haenyeo: The Breath of Jeju (Special Exhibition)
Event Period
									Aug 17, 2026
										 - 
									Nov 20, 2026
						The Korean Cultural Center Washington, D.C. (KCCDC) in collaboration with the Jeju Special Self-Governing Province proudly presents Haenyeo: The Breath of Jeju, a new special exhibition of authentic equipment, activities, informational panels, videos, and books exploring the world of Jeju island's famous women free divers knows as Haenyeo (해녀), on view August 17 through November 20, 2026. This exhibition highlights the  unique local culture of Haenyeo, which was inscribed on UNESCO’s Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2016. Visitors can explore the lives of Jeju’s women divers, their unique diving practice known as muljil, their sustainable way of living in harmony with the sea, and the strong community tradition built on cooperation and the sharing of knowledge across generations. Featuring authentic diving suits and tools, along with books, videos, and hands-on activities, the exhibition offers visitors an opportunity to discover the cultural heritage and community spirit of the Jeju Haenyeo. Dates: August 17 – November 20, 2026 Location: Korean Cultural Center Washington, D.C., First Floor No RSVP needed! Please drop by during regular visitor hours.

Hanji: Embracing Color featuring works by artist Lee Seung-chul
Event Period
									Aug 20, 2026
										 - 
									Nov 20, 2026
						The Korean Cultural Center Washington, D.C. (KCCDC) proudly presents Hanji: Embracing Color, a new exhibition of works by artist Lee Seung-chul that explores the chromatic and sculptural possibilities of hanji, traditional Korean paper, while also tracing its deep cultural roots as a hand-made medium for both craft and communication.   On view August 20 through November 20 with a public pre-opening reception on Wednesday, August 19, the exhibition presents a dizzying array of hanji (한지)-based artworks characterized by their tactile physicality, sculptural presence, and vibrant, nature-derived colors.   Buddhist sculptural forms in both gold and stone-like hues, floating multicolor moon jars evocative of classical porcelain dalhangari (달항아리), and even fragments of bandaji (반닫이) chest furniture all adorn the gallery floors, walls, and ceilings, alongside the artist’s bojagi (보자기) wrapping cloth textile works that feature the same traditional natural dyes. The exhibition also highlights those traditional natural colors derived from plant- and mineral-based pigments that are applied to hanji without obscuring the underlying material qualities. Lee’s works, based on more than 40 years of study and conservation research, are thus elevated visually as they convey the richness of Korea’s distinctive color palette and its aesthetic sensibility.   Also presented in the exhibition are historical examples of hanji collected by the artist over many years, including books and official papers that trace their roots back to Korea’s ancient Joseon Dynasty, offering visitors a broader understanding of hanji’s history and cultural significance. For centuries in Korea, hanji was far more than mere paper for writing—it was used in architecture, handicrafts, painting, preservation, and the creation of everyday objects with great utility, while also serving as one of Korea’s most enduring cultural heritage materials. Renowned for its distinctive texture, durability, and textured, expressive qualities, hanji continues to inspire contemporary artistic practices. In recent years, its cultural significance and traditional craftsmanship have also gained international recognition.   On a deeper level, Lee’s hanji works explore the distant origins of Korea’s traditional culture and visual language. As he researches the material qualities of hanji, Lee reinterprets iconic Korean cultural forms like the Bodhisattva of wisdom or moon jar, both revered through the ages for their beauty and majesty. Rather than simply reproducing such revered forms from Korea’s cultural heritage, Lee uses hanji—a material shaped by centuries of history and tradition—to reawaken the historical significance of these cultural icons.   Hanji: Embracing Color launches with a pre-opening reception on Wednesday A
