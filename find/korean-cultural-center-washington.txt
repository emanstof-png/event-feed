K-FOOD Exhibition Space
Event Period
									Nov 24, 2026
										 - 
									Feb 28, 2027
						The Korean Cultural Center Washington, D.C. (KCCDC) is pleased to introduce its newly opened K-FOOD exhibition space, located on the first floor of the Cultural Center. The exhibition highlights the richness and diversity of Korean culinary culture, from traditional Korean earthenware (onggi), fermented pastes and sauces (jang), and kimchi to a wide variety of Korean dishes and snacks. Visitors can explore a range of exhibits, including remarkably lifelike kimchi replicas, a traditional Korean refreshments table crafted by a Korean artist, and Korean tableware, offering a glimpse into the artistry and traditions behind Korean food culture. We invite you to visit our new K-FOOD exhibition space and discover the unique flavors, traditions, and cultural heritage of Korean cuisine, all in one place!

Jungjin Lee: In Resonance
Event Period
									Dec 03, 2026
										 - 
									Feb 19, 2027
						The Korean Cultural Center Washington, D.C. (KCCDC) proudly presents Jungjin Lee: In Resonance, a new exhibition spanning decades of photographic works by celebrated Korean artist Jungjin Lee, on view December 3, 2026 – February 19, 2027. Featuring works drawn from nine of Lee’s series, many presented on richly textured traditional Korean hanji paper using a unique emulsion technique, Lee’s work often confronts the question of how a photograph, in all its stillness, might carry the force of an encounter. For decades, Lee has travelled the world in pursuit of those impactful personal encounters with unique locations, from Korea’s Ulleungdo island to the Middle East and the American west. Her striking images of seemingly desolate natural locations and isolated cultural sites may at first seem sparse, stark, and unnerving. But by recreating certain works on traditional Korean hanji, which absorbs ink and imbues tangible texture, she strives to recreate those original moments of awe-inspiring fullness in a visceral way. Blending austerity, mystery, and a richness of negative space, Lee’s work evokes both a deeply Korean aesthetic and a universal sense of wonder at the breathtaking beauty of the world. Jungjin Lee: In Resonance, guest curated by JuWon Park, will remain on view and open to the public at the KCCDC December 3, 2026 through February 19, 2027, including an opening reception on Thursday, December 3 at 6:00 p.m. For more, see below or visit the artist’s website at www.jungjinlee.com. Jungjin Lee: In Resonance December 3, 2026 - February 19, 2027 Opening Reception: Thursday, December 3 at 6:00 p.m.  Korean Cultural Center Washington, D.C.  RSVP HERE Exhibition Statement by Guest Curator JuWon Park Curator Contact Info: jupark1010@gmail.com There is a persistent search that runs through the stillness of Jungjin Lee’s photographs. For four decades, Lee has turned to photography to pursue questions about the nature of existence. That impulse has taken her from an isolated village on the Korean island of Ulleungdo to the barren deserts of the American West, the contested terrain of the West Bank, the wetlands of Florida, and the volcanic coasts of Iceland. Where the land appears empty, she senses an overwhelming fullness. Lee’s work begins in moments of heightened attention. Whether contemplating a vast landscape or a familiar object, she registers an unfamiliar intensity in ordinary forms. In their austerity, her subjects evoke unease and a sense of mystery that seems to exceed what the eye can discern. Photography becomes a means of giving shape to these experiences, of making tangible what remains difficult to grasp or articulate. This pursuit continues in the physical process of making her works. What Lee absorbs bodily in the field is reconstituted through gradations of black and white, negative space, and the textured surface of hanji (Korean mulberry paper). In the 1990s, Lee began hand-coating large sheets of hanji with a photographic emulsion called Liquid Light, a laborious and unpredictable process she has likened to a form of meditation.
