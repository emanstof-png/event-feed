Patricia Williams: Transformations and Importations
Patricia Williams explores the concept that creative ideas are catching. Thus, a secular love song from 1600, or thereabouts, finds its way via a Christian passion hymn scored by Bach in 1656 and a protest song by Tom Glazer in 1947 to Paul Simon’s
 Bridge Over Troubled Water
 in 1970 and 
American Tune
 in 1973. Ms. Williams further transforms the music into visual art. Some paintings in this series interpret the notes of the musical score as shape and color, while others express the music's emotional and intellectual content.
Gale Wallar: Parallelismus
The work in this exhibition by Gale Wallar reflects 
parallelismus,
 using formal organizational principles (contrast, repetition, emphasis, proportion, and balance) and sensory elements (color, line, value, mass, and space).  It emphasizes the symmetrical alignments found in nature that provide order in a chaotic world. The Swiss artist Ferdinand Hodler defined 
parallelismus 
as a visual philosophy: a process of simplification, balance and repetition found in nature; the fundamental order of the universe.

As part of our 50 for 50 series, Touchstone is featuring a weeklong spotlight exhibition by Keith Kozloff. Other late fall exhibitions will also be on view.
Keith Kozloff’s objective with this photo series is to visually suggest layers of Irish landscape’s history. The typical act of photographing freezes a single moment in time through the click of the camera’s shutter, but for these works he uses multiple exposure photography to disturb and compress time’s arrow. The resulting semi-abstract compositions can be initially disorienting. The apparent solidity of the landscape becomes attenuated.

As part of our 50 for 50 series, Touchstone is featuring a weeklong spotlight exhibition by Jayne Gaskins. Other late fall exhibitions will also be on view.
Gaskins’s practice is deeply personal and intimate. Her compositions feature hard edges and intense colors that mirror the strength and complexity of human feelings. She welcomes viewers to bring their own perspectives to her work, recognizing that emotional responses are inherently individual. For Gaskins, a work is successful if it inspires viewers to embark on their own introspective journeys, just as creating the work has led her through one of her own. Her artistic journey remains ongoing.

As part of our 50 for 50 series, Touchstone is featuring a weeklong spotlight exhibition by Kwaku Ofori-Yirenkyi, also known as Absku. And get a first look at all of the late fall Touchstone exhibitions! 
Drawing from the experience of a Ghanaian raised in America, Absku explores migration, memory, identity, and belonging through the lens of the third-culture experience. Combining portraiture, abstraction, African textiles, Adinkra symbolism, and tactile mixed-media surfaces, their work examines how culture transforms across borders and generations. Through layered textures, fragmented figures, and sculptural elements, the artist presents identity as fluid, continually negotiated, and ever evolving.
